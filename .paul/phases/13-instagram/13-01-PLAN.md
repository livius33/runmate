<objective>
## Goal
An Instagram account for Runmate that posts four times a week without Livius writing or designing anything: a
pipeline drafts each post from material that already exists, renders the images, puts a week of posts in front of
Livius for approval, publishes the approved ones through the official API and learns from the numbers.

## Purpose
Livius asked (2026-11-21) for an Instagram account "with an automatic content machine behind it that does the
marketing". The app is German and the first market is German-speaking runners, who are on Instagram and Strava far
more than on Hacker News or Reddit, where the launch drafts in docs/launch/ANNOUNCE.md are aimed.
</objective>

<context>
## What the machine can draw on (all of it exists today)

- `methodologies/research/*.md` (13 notes: long run, paces, taper, load cycles, adaptation …) and the eight method
  templates. Every number in them has a source. This is the main well: one note yields 10–20 posts.
- The demo mode (`/demo`): a complete account with invented data. Screenshots from it show the product without
  anybody's real runs.
- Merged pull requests and the engine rules: "what changed this week" and "why the plan moved your long run".
- A calendar of the big German-language races (Berlin, Hamburg, Frankfurt, Köln, Wien, Zürich …): "16 weeks to
  Hamburg: a block starts on Monday".
- The design tokens and components of the app, so cards look like the product and not like a template pack.

## Content pillars

| Pillar | Share | Format | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|--------|
| Method explained ("Warum der lange Lauf bei 32 km endet") | 2 of 4 | Carousel, 5–7 cards | research notes |
| The plan reacts ("Langer Lauf verpasst. Was jetzt?") | 1 of 4 | Carousel or single card with real screenshots | demo mode + adaptation.md |
| Open and in progress (new feature, rule change, the code is public) | 1 of 4, alternating with race calendar | Single card | merged PRs, race calendar |

Every post is also published as a story (business accounts can publish stories through the API). Reels are a later
step: they need motion templates (screen recordings of the demo), and a carousel pipeline that works comes first.

## How the machine works

```
sources ──▶ planner ──▶ fact check ──▶ renderer ──▶ approval ──▶ publisher ──▶ insights
(notes,     (Claude,    (numbers       (cards as    (Livius,     (Instagram    (reach, saves,
 PRs,        weekly)     must be in     PNG 4:5)     weekly)      API, cron)    link clicks)
 calendar,               the source)                                                 │
 demo)          ▲                                                                    │
                └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Planner** (weekly, Sunday): reads the sources and the list of what was already posted, and writes five post
   briefs as JSON: pillar, hook, text per card, caption, hashtags, and for every claim the file and line it comes
   from. Model: `claude-sonnet-5-5` through the Claude API; a week costs cents.
2. **Fact check** (code, not a model): every number and every named method in a card must appear in the cited
   source lines. A brief that fails is dropped, not repaired by hand. No user counts, no testimonials, no medical
   promises ("verletzungsfrei", "garantiert") — a word list rejects them.
3. **Renderer**: React templates → PNG at 1080 × 1350 via `next/og`, which the repo already uses for its Open Graph
   images. Product screenshots come from Playwright against the demo mode. No generated photos of runners: they
   look generic, and realistic AI images need a label under Article 50 of the EU AI Act (in force since
   2026-08-02).
4. **Approval**: one private page per week with the five posts as they will look. Livius approves, edits the
   caption or rejects each one; four go out. Nothing is published that was not approved. This is also what keeps
   the captions out of the labelling duty: AI-drafted text needs no label when a person has reviewed it and takes
   editorial responsibility.
5. **Publisher** (cron, Tue/Thu/Sat/Sun 18:30 Europe/Berlin): Instagram API with Instagram Login — create the
   media container from a public image URL, wait until it is ready, publish, then the story. The limit is 100
   API posts per rolling 24 hours; this plan uses fewer than two a day. The access token lives 60 days and is
   refreshed by a weekly job.
6. **Insights** (weekly): reach, saves, shares, profile visits per post go back into the planner prompt ("more
   like these three, fewer like these two"). The link in the bio carries `?utm_source=instagram`, so sign-ups
   from Instagram show up in the site analytics.

## What stays manual, on purpose

- Automatic likes, follows, comments or direct messages. Meta's terms forbid them and accounts get closed for it.
- Answering comments and messages. The pipeline can draft replies; Livius sends them.
- Paid ads. Not part of this plan.

## Where it lives

A private repository `runmate-marketing`, not this public one: the queue, unpublished drafts and the insights
are nobody else's business, and the Instagram token belongs in that repo's secrets. It reads this repo as a
checkout. Scheduling by GitHub Actions cron; images on a public blob store, because Instagram fetches them by URL.

## Decisions Livius has to make

1. Handle. Candidates: `runmate.dev`, `runmate.app`, `getrunmate`, `runmate.laufen`. Availability shows only in
   the sign-up form.
2. Language: German only (recommended: the app is German), or German with a monthly English post.
3. Approval: every post (recommended for the first eight weeks), or publish automatically after 48 hours without
   a veto. Without review, AI-drafted posts carry the `is_ai_generated` flag.
4. Private repository for the pipeline: yes or no.
5. Start date. Not before runmate.dev answers and the Impressum is filled in: the link in the bio would lead to a
   404, and a commercial German account needs an Impressum that is reachable in two taps.

## Livius' part (about 30 minutes, once)

- Create the Instagram account, switch it to a business account, turn on two-factor sign-in.
- Create a Meta developer app with "Instagram API with Instagram Login", add the account as a tester, generate
  the long-lived token and store it as a repository secret (`IG_ACCESS_TOKEN`, `IG_USER_ID`). Whether publishing
  to one's own account needs App Review is to be checked in this step; for a tester account in development mode
  it should not.
- An `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` as a repository secret.

<acceptance_criteria>
AC-1  A dry run produces a week of five posts (cards as PNG, caption, sources) from the repo alone, with no account
      and no token.
AC-2  The fact check rejects a brief with a number that is not in its source, and one with a forbidden claim
      (tests).
AC-3  Nothing can be published that is not marked approved (test against a mocked API).
AC-4  With the token set: one approved carousel and its story are published to the account; a failed container
      is retried once and then reported, never published twice.
AC-5  The token refresh runs weekly and a refresh that fails opens an issue in the private repo.
AC-6  Twelve seed posts (three weeks) exist and are approved before the first one goes out.
AC-7  No secret in either repo: gitleaks green, the token only in repository secrets.
</acceptance_criteria>

<tasks>
1. Checkpoint with Livius: the five decisions above.
2. Templates and renderer: six card layouts (title, statement, number, comparison, screenshot, closing card with
   the handle), light and dark, from the app's tokens. Dry run on three research notes.
3. Planner and fact check, with the list of posted topics so nothing repeats inside eight weeks.
4. Approval page and queue (JSON files in the private repo; the page reads them).
5. Twelve seed posts, approved by Livius.
6. Checkpoint with Livius: account and Meta app exist, secrets are set.
7. Publisher, story publishing and token refresh; first live post.
8. Insights job and the feedback into the planner. Review after four weeks: what worked, whether Reels are next.
</tasks>

<boundaries>
- No engagement automation of any kind.
- No invented numbers, quotes or user stories; no health promises.
- No real user data in a post, the demo account only.
- No post before the account's link target and the Impressum are live.
- The public repo gets no token, no queue and no drafts.
</boundaries>
